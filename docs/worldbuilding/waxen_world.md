# PART I — VOICE GALLERY

The teller from §8: native to the Cerement, second person, past tense, finds nothing strange. Never names the secret; the wrongness rides in on an idiom or a habit, and the reader connects what the narrator never does. Write the rest of the game against these.

**Crossing into a thin place**
> Past the eighth milestone the road got generous — wider than anyone had cut it, the ruts gone smooth, the hills to either side poured into that even, dreamless shape you'd learned not to walk into after dark. The carter turned the mule without a word, and you paid him full fare for the half-trip, because that was the custom and because he was right to.

**One that came back**
> Vhessa's boy came back on the third night, the way they did when you hadn't been thorough, and stood at the gate saying the small true things he'd always said — that the well-rope wanted replacing, that his mother kept the good knife dull. She gave him bread through the bars and did not open the gate. By the grey hour he'd wandered off toward the low ground, still talking. She swept the step where he'd stood, and that was the end of it. You learned not to ask whose name a person used when they thanked you.

**The age of a bridge**
> You asked the old woman how long the bridge had stood and she said it had always stood, which is what they all said about anything that mattered. The toll-book went back eleven years and then stopped at a clean first page, the way toll-books did, the name at its head in a hand no one living wrote. You paid your half-penny and crossed. It held. Most things held that you didn't ask too closely after.

**A lord passing**
> The one they called the Penurious kept court three streets up, though all you ever met of him was the cold — the lamps along the Procession sinking low as his chair went by and coming back grudging after, the warming-beggars gone to other walls. They said he hadn't eaten in an age and had no need to. You tipped your head as the chair passed, the way everyone did, felt the year go a little shorter, and carried on to your meeting.

**A thing worth stupid money**
> What the digger sold you was a hinge, just a hinge, but it had a weight that argued with your hand and an edge that hadn't gone soft in all the years it should have. Set on the table, it made the table look like the lie. The old families paid stupid money for pieces like that and kept them behind glass and wouldn't say why, and you sold it on inside the day — because a thing that real makes everything near it look thin, and you had to sleep in that room.

**Good company**
> You'd have sworn you'd never met her, and yet she knew the back way out of the rookery and which of the Ssemnaar brothers to pay, and she fell into step like the road had been holding her place. She was good company on a bad week. Later you went to remember who'd named her to you and found you couldn't, and decided it didn't matter — the way you decided a great many things that year.

**The man with the soft hand**
> The man you hired to open the strongbox did it with no key and no sound, and you saw why the trade was poorly thought of: when he was done his hand wouldn't take the lamplight right, gave it back too smooth, a candle's hand where a man's should be. He worked his fingers, drew his glove on, and named a price that was mostly drink. They don't last, that trade. You paid him and didn't shake on it.

*What each buries, for your reference only:* a Deliquescence; a returned dead and borrowed memory (Palingenesis); the shallow false past (Lethe); a Castellan and the Dimming (Penury); an authentic Aevum-relic against the surrounding forgery; a Director-placed Corollary; a sorcerer tallowing (extenuation). The player is told none of this.

# PART II — GENERATORS

Everything below is a **seed**, not canon. The point is abstraction: the Director instantiates specifics from these schemas per playthrough, so the world is consistent without being fixed. Each generator derives from the cosmology, so its output is native by construction.

## A. The generative spine — Seam-Stance

One axis threads people, factions, and history: **a thing's relationship to the seams of the forgery.** Roll it for any agent or group; it is the root parameter everything else hangs from.

| Stance | Knows? | Behaves | Rarity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oblivious** | no | props the forgery up by habit; explains nothing | the mass of everyone |
| **Uneasy** | senses it | superstitious; rituals and avoidances that encode the truth without stating it | common |
| **Collaborator** | yes | *defends* the dream; fears waking; serves a Castellan | rare, powerful |
| **Wrought** | enough to use it | exploits the thinning for power; corrupting, short-lived | rare |
| **Waked** | yes | wants out, or wants others out; hunted | very rare |

The player is the anomaly: a soul whose **Scintilla** is stirring while their Stance is still nominally Oblivious. That gap is the game.

## B. Faction generator

A faction is generated as a product of four axes, then snapped to the nearest archetype and named.

- **Stance** (§A) — sets motive and how they read to the forensic player.
- **Entangled Law** — one of the seven Caryatids. The concealment-law the faction serves, exploits, or unknowingly maintains. This supplies *flavor and method*.
- **Material base** — the scarce thing they control: lamp-oil/heat (Penury), records/witnesses/memory (Lethe), true-relics (Aevum bleed-through), safe passage through the thinning (the Asymptote), the returned dead (Palingenesis), fragments of true language (the Lacuna), or plain debt and coin.
- **Cohesion** — what binds them: blood, oath, a shared secret, debt-bondage, addiction to extenuation, or a relic they guard.

**Archetype templates** (the generator clusters toward these; each has parameter slots):

- **Keepers** — *Oblivious × any Law × habitual maintenance.* Guilds that run a concealment-law without knowing it: lamplighters (Penury), toll- and record-wardens (Lethe), road-surveyors (the Asymptote). The forgery's unwitting immune system.
- **A House of the Seven** — *Collaborator × one Law × worship.* One cult-family per Castellan; they want the dream to continue and act to keep it asleep. Antagonist-rich.
- **Tallow-Orders** — *Wrought × relics or the Lacuna.* Decadent antiquarian sorcerers trafficking in extenuation and true-relics. Aristocratic, rotting, never numerous.
- **Lectors** — *Waked × the Lacuna.* Hunted cells holding fragments of true language; they work toward the Matins. The player's likeliest allies, and the riskiest.
- **Undertakings** — *any Stance × Palingenesis.* Trades built entirely around managing the returned: thorough-burners, gate-wardens, those who employ or wall out the come-back-wrong.
- **Render-folk** — *Uneasy/Wrought × frontier.* Scavengers and the half-tallowed who live at the thinning edge and sell passage and salvage.

**Procedure:** roll the four axes → snap to archetype → self-name in plain words (a Keeper guild calls itself something mundane and true-sounding); name any individuals via the §12 grammar.

*Sample output:* `Oblivious × Lethe × records × oath` → **the Quiet Office**, a guild of toll- and ledger-wardens who, without knowing, ensure no book reaches past its allowed depth; bound by a clerk's oath sworn on a founder none of them can name.

> **[HOOK]** Factions are Director-instantiated from these axes. Stance sets whether a faction reads as threat, mark, or ally, and whether its members are plausible Corollary-sources. Entangled Law tells the perception layer which *tell* this faction radiates.

## C. Society generator (per settlement, no fixed map)

A settlement's character is set by how four cosmological pressures land *locally*. Roll each:

- **Scarcity (Penury):** what is rationed here → sets class and status (who controls the dwindling — oil, grain, heat, salt, clean water).
- **Amnesia (Lethe):** how deep memory reaches here → sets how legitimacy is claimed. With no founding myth available, authority rests on present control or on a *forged* lineage everyone agrees not to test.
- **Return (Palingenesis):** how the returned are handled here → a custom: burn thoroughly, wall them out, put them to work, or quietly venerate them.
- **The Unsayable (Lacuna):** the local taboo → the one thing not named here, and the euphemism used instead.

*Sample output:* a salt-town where status is salt-rights; the ruling family's "ancient" charter is plainly thirty years old and no one says so; the returned are given a day's work and then sent down-valley; and no one speaks of the lake, only of "the low water."

> **[HOOK]** Settlement-gen seeds POIs. The local return-custom and the local taboo are pre-placed tells the player can read; the forged lineage is a ready-made provenance puzzle.

## D. History generator (strata, not a timeline)

The cosmology forbids a real chronology — Lethe eats the past, and the deep past may be forged backdrop. So history generates as **layers of decreasing reliability**, not a sequence of dates. Fill each stratum per region:

1. **the Lived** (~a generation) — reliable, remembered, the stuff of current contracts.
2. **the Hearsay** (older) — Lethe-thinned, contradictory; names without faces, deeds without dates.
3. **the Ruin** (the forger-empires) — grand, decadent, and *ambiguous*: some authentic Aevum bleed-through, some Director set-dressing painted to look deep. The richest forensic surface.
4. **the Unwritten** (before the casting) — no records, only the Lacuna. Inferable, never recovered diegetically.

The deeper the stratum, the less the Chronicle will confirm, and the more a "fact" about it is really a verdict.

> **[HOOK]** Each stratum carries a reliability parameter the Chronicle honors. "Is this ruin authentic Aevum or forged depth?" is a top-tier provenance verdict — the core verb pointed at history itself.

## E. People generator

A person is your existing **larva** (the Lie/Want/Need engine) plus a short set of **cosmological flags** that perturb their tells and dialogue. Most people roll Oblivious with no flags; the rare flags make the memorable ones.

- **Seam-Stance** (§A) — how they talk about the wrong things, or refuse to.
- **Entangled Law** — which Caryatid's tell marks them: a Lethe-marked person loses threads mid-sentence; an Effigies-marked person is uncannily consistent; a Concord-marked person never contradicts themselves even when they should.
- **Returned?** (Palingenesis) — died and came back wrong: borrowed memories, uncanny accuracy on small true things, a name that doesn't quite fit.
- **Tallowing?** (Wrought) — has extenuated; smoothing out; a hand that takes the light wrong.
- **Corollary?** (Director-placed) — the established flag: the player may suspect, never confirm.
- **Scintilla state** — dormant / stirring / risen. Almost universally dormant.

**Procedure:** larva-engine generates the surface; flags are cheap annotations that bias what the perception layer surfaces. A flagged person isn't *described* as flagged — the tell leaks through the larva's ordinary self-presentation, per the §8 voice.

*Sample output:* an innkeeper, `Oblivious larva + Returned + Lethe-marked` — warm, helpful, slightly too accurate about your business, who cannot tell you how long he has kept the place and grows uneasy if pressed.

> **[HOOK]** Flags are Chronicle annotations rendered as tells, never as labels. The player's verb is reading the flags *through* the self-presentation — the same forensic act as the rest of the game, at the scale of a single face.
